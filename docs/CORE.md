## PREAMBLE

This document defines the foundational laws of AVCS — Adaptive Vector Control System.

These laws are operational. They describe the structural conditions under which a system remains under control, and the conditions under which control is lost.

They are derived from the AVCS Charter v1.1 and are consistent with it. Where a law and the Charter appear to conflict, the Charter prevails.

The laws are numbered but not ordered by priority. Each is foundational.

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Any system can be interrupted before it becomes uncontrollable.

---

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

**Control = the ability to stop in time.**

If a system cannot be stopped, it is not under control.

---

## 3. THE FAILURE CONDITION

Systems do not fail when something breaks.

They fail when:

- signals are visible but do not trigger action,
- deviation does not require justification,
- continuation becomes automatic,
- stopping becomes uncertain.

**Loss of control happens before failure becomes visible.**

---

## 4. RED ZONE LAW

If stopping is uncertain → continuation is prohibited.

This is non-negotiable.

---

## 5. INTEGRITY EQUATION

**Integrity = STOP × North × Field**

If any element = 0 → integrity collapses.

The equation is multiplicative, not additive. A system does not compensate for a missing STOP with a stronger North. A system does not compensate for a contested North with a wider Field.

All three are required. Loss of any one is sufficient for collapse.

---

## 6. NORTH IS GUARDED

The Compass function has **veto authority** over any decision that violates North.

It does not manage operations. It overrides them when necessary.

North is not interpreted. North is applied.

---

## 7. WHAT AVCS IS

AVCS is a structural control system for decision integrity under pressure.

It operates at the layer between information and controlled consequence.

It does not replace risk management. It does not replace operational expertise. It does not replace human judgment.

It examines whether the architecture surrounding critical decisions is structurally capable of turning knowledge into controlled, authorized, and reviewable action.

---

## 8. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

AVCS operates through four complementary modules:

```text
SIM diagnoses  →  INS navigates  →  HPSM stabilises  →  Playbook executes
SIM (Structural Integrity Module) — diagnoses decision architecture.

INS (Intelligence Navigation System) — maintains orientation under pressure.

HPSM (Human Performance Stability Model) — protects human judgment.

Playbook (Execution Playbook) — converts intent into controlled action.

These modules are not optional. Absence of any one results in loss of control.

Diagnostic application may begin with SIM and expand as needed. Full operational architecture requires all four.

9. CONTROL IS NOT PERFORMANCE
Performance ≠ Control.

A system may achieve results while already being out of control.

Performance is measured. Control is proven by the ability to stop.

10. SYSTEM CONDITIONS OF CONTROL
A system is under control only if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

Triggers are mandatory — critical deviations activate escalation, not interpretation.

Ownership is singular — one accountable owner, present in real time.

Intervention is protected — STOP can be exercised without structural penalty.

Overrides are transparent — deviations are visible, logged, traceable.

Drift is detected — the accumulation of small deviations is tracked.

North is determinate — the boundary is applicable, not contested.

Authority is explicit — recommendation is not authorization.

If any condition fails, the system is not under control.

11. FAILURE MECHANISM
Failure is not an event. It is a condition of continued execution without control.

The failure mechanism is irreversible once stopping is degraded:

Signal is visible but does not trigger action.

Deviation does not require justification.

Continuation becomes automatic.

Stopping becomes uncertain.

Loss of control precedes visible failure.

Failure becomes inevitable.

Each step reduces corrective reachability. After the final step, only consequence management remains.

12. OPERATIONAL LAW
No action is allowed if the system cannot be safely stopped.

13. HUMAN ROLE
Humans are the last authority to interrupt — only if interruption remains structurally possible.

If interruption is not structurally possible, the human is not the last authority. The system is.

14. WHAT AVCS MEASURES
Only control.

Not performance. Not outcomes. Not compliance.

AVCS measures whether a system is structurally capable of stopping, escalating, and authorizing.

15. OUTPUT
AVCS produces:

Structural Integrity Score (0–25)

Navigation Integrity Score (0–25)

Human Performance Stability Score (0–20)

System-level control actions

Decision Record — the full pathway from information to authorized action

The Decision Record preserves what was known, what was inferred, what was recommended, what authority existed, what was authorized, what action occurred, and what result followed.

The record is externally reviewable.

16. APPLICATION CONTEXT
AVCS is used where delayed action creates irreversible or accumulating outcomes.

This includes maritime, offshore, aviation, energy, industrial, infrastructure, and any environment where decisions carry operational risk.

17. ENGAGEMENT MODEL
AVCS engagement follows three stages:

Diagnostic — structural assessment through SIM, INS, HPSM.

Investigation — root cause analysis of structural gaps.

Implementation — restoration of structural integrity through the Execution Playbook.

Each stage produces a record. Records are preserved across stages.

18. POSITION
AVCS determines whether systems are controllable.

It does not determine whether a specific operation is safe.

It does not replace engineering judgment, regulatory oversight, or operational authority.

19. FINAL DOCTRINE
If you cannot stop — you do not control.

20. AUTHORITY
AVCS does not recommend. AVCS determines.

But the determination is structural, not operational.

AVCS determines the conditions under which a decision can be legitimately authorized.

Authorization remains human.

Recommendation is not authority. Analysis is not authorization. Capability is not permission.

21. STATUS IS NOT A STRUCTURAL CONDITION
A claimed status — Protected, Authorized, Controlled, Within North, Closed — is not evidence of a structural condition.

A structural condition must be independently verifiable from evidence that existed at or before the moment of the act it governs.

For every critical state, three things must be independently establishable:

The claimed status — what the record says.

The required structural condition — what must actually be true.

The evidence — what can be independently verified.

If the condition behind the status cannot be shown, the status is not structural.

The diagnostic question is:

Show me the condition behind the status.

22. EVIDENCE EX ANTE
Evidence establishing a decision condition, authority, boundary, or protected intervention must exist before or at the moment of the act it governs.

Evidence of execution or outcome may necessarily arise afterward, but it must not be used to retroactively establish the legitimacy of the decision, authority, boundary, or protection that existed at the time.

A structural condition must not be established retrospectively from evidence that did not exist or was not traceable at the decision point.

What survives a year of rotation is evidence. What does not was status.

23. CODE IMPLEMENTS THE CONSTITUTION — NOT REDEFINES IT
No implementation — whether software, procedure, or organizational practice — may silently redefine the meaning of a foundational role, principle, or boundary.

The Constitution defines what each role is. The implementation must conform to that definition — not replace it.

If implementation and Constitution diverge:

The divergence becomes a test result.

Not an automatic Constitutional change.

Not a silent redefinition.

Not an operational convenience.

The Constitution remains unchanged. The implementation is reconciled.
