## PREAMBLE

This Field Guide is the practical handbook for AVCS Practitioners conducting SIM audits.

It implements the requirements of the **Code of Practice v1.1**. It operates under the ethical framework of the **Code of Ethics v1.1**.

Where this Guide and the Code of Practice appear to conflict, the **Code of Practice prevails**.

This is the work of an AVCS Practitioner.

**Not an inspector.**
**Not a policeman.**

## PHASE 1 — PREPARATION

### 1.1 Collect Initial Information

The Practitioner requests the following documents **at least two weeks** before the visit:

| Document Type | What to Look For |
|---|---|
| **Policies & Procedures** | HSE policy, risk management procedures, escalation protocols, stop-work authority. |
| **Deviation Logs** | Incident logs, near-miss reports, records of procedural breaches (last 6–12 months). |
| **Organisational Structure** | Org chart, responsibility matrix, job descriptions for key roles. |
| **Previous Audit Reports** | Internal and external audit findings (HSE, ISO 45001, etc.). |
| **Training Records** | Competency checks, training attendance, safety briefings. |

### 1.2 Audit Plan

The Practitioner prepares a plan covering:

- **Objective:** assess the structural integrity of the decision-making system.
- **Scope:** one site / business unit / process.
- **Schedule:** 2–3 days on site.
- **Key respondents:** operators, supervisors, HSE staff, line managers, senior leaders.
- **Documents to review on site:** shift logs, meeting minutes, incident investigation files.

### 1.3 Conflict of Interest Check

The Practitioner must verify that no conflict of interest exists, including recent employment (within the preceding two years).

This is required by the **Code of Ethics v1.1, Section 3.2**.

## PHASE 2 — FIELDWORK

### 2.1 Opening Meeting

**Attendees:** Practitioner, site manager, HSE manager, representatives from key departments.

**Agenda:**

- Introduce the Practitioner and the mission (not an "inspection", but a "diagnostic").
- Explain SIM methodology and objectives.
- Confirm the schedule and plan.
- Request any missing documents.
- Discuss confidentiality (anonymity of interviews).

**Key message:**

> "We are not here to evaluate people. We are here to evaluate the system they work in. Our goal is to see where the system might quietly fail — so you can fix it before something happens."

### 2.2 Document Review

The Practitioner studies the provided materials, looking for evidence related to each pillar:

| Pillar | What to Look For in Documents |
|---|---|
| **Trigger Clarity** | Are escalation thresholds clearly documented? Are there cases where deviations were recorded but no action was taken? |
| **Decision Ownership** | Is a single accountable owner defined for critical decisions? Is this visible in procedures? |
| **Protected Intervention** | Is stop-work authority formally documented? Are there records of work stoppages and how they were handled? |
| **Override Transparency** | How are procedural deviations recorded? Who authorises them? Are they visible in reports? |
| **Drift Detection** | Is there any trend analysis of repeated minor deviations? Are recurring issues flagged? |

### 2.3 Evidence Verification

For every claimed status — **Protected, Authorized, Controlled, Within North, Closed** — the Practitioner must attempt to identify the **condition behind the status**.

If the condition cannot be shown from evidence, the pillar is scored as **structurally absent**.

This is required by:

- **Charter v1.1, Section 4.3**
- **CORE v2.1, Law 21**
- **Code of Ethics v1.1, Section 11**
- **Code of Practice v1.1, Section 3**
- **SIM v1.1, Section 2.3**

### 2.4 Interviews

#### 2.4.1 Who to Interview

| Group | Number | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Operators, technicians** | 3–5 | Understand real practices, pressure, fear, informal rules. |
| **Supervisors, shift leads** | 2–3 | See how procedures work in the transition zone (field ↔ management). |
| **HSE personnel** | 1–2 | Assess their role, influence, and support from leadership. |
| **Line managers** | 2–3 | Understand how they perceive their responsibility and authority. |
| **Senior leaders** | 1–2 | Gauge strategic commitment and structural awareness. |

#### 2.4.2 Interview Format

- **Duration:** 45–60 minutes.
- **Format:** one-on-one, confidential.
- **Notes:** Practitioner takes notes (no names, only roles).

#### 2.4.3 Protection of Informants

Before each interview, the Practitioner must:

- explain the anonymity protection (Code of Ethics v1.1, Section 1.3),
- explain that no identifying information will enter the report,
- explain that if a manager requests attribution, the Practitioner will refuse and cite the Code of Ethics,
- confirm that the informant understands and consents.

Where an informant may be in a vulnerable position (e.g., contract worker, recent hire, individual under performance review), the Practitioner must explicitly warn of potential risks before proceeding.

#### 2.4.4 Sample Questions by Pillar

**Pillar 1: Trigger Clarity**

- "Tell me about the last time you noticed something was off. What did you do?"
- "Are there clear rules for when to raise the alarm or stop work? Or is it up to the individual?"
- "Has there ever been a time when you saw a problem but didn't know who to tell?"

**Pillar 2: Decision Ownership**

- "Have you ever waited for a decision that no one seemed to own?"
- "During abnormal conditions, who has final decision authority?"
- "Is that person physically present during operations?"

**Pillar 3: Protected Intervention**

- "Do you feel you can stop work if you see a risk?"
- "What happens to someone who stops work? How is it viewed?"
- "Does stopping work affect your pay, bonus, or career?"
- "Is there a formal way to refuse duty if you are unfit (fatigue, illness, distress)?"

**Pillar 4: Override Transparency**

- "Do procedures ever get bent to meet targets?"
- "How is that documented? Or does it stay informal?"
- "Who usually decides to bypass a procedure?"

**Pillar 5: Drift Detection**

- "Are there small recurring issues that never seem to get fixed?"
- "Does anyone track these patterns?"
- "Are there things done 'not by the book' that everyone has just gotten used to?"

#### 2.4.5 What Else to Notice

- Body language, hesitations, evasive answers.
- Non-verbal signs of fear or uncertainty.
- Gaps between what managers say and what operators say.

**Structural integrity gaps appear in inconsistency.**

### 2.5 Handling Discrepancies

When statements from different groups contradict each other, the Practitioner must:

1. **Record** the discrepancy as a separate observation — not resolve it silently.
2. **Attribute** the discrepancy to the pillar(s) to which it relates.
3. **Reflect** the discrepancy in the Vulnerability Map.
4. **Report** the discrepancy without aligning to either side.
5. **Verify** any claimed status against documentation.

The discrepancy itself is a **finding**.

It is not resolved in favor of either party.

It is preserved as a structural signal.

### 2.6 Workplace Observations

The Practitioner spends time in the field, observing:

- how procedures are actually followed (not just on paper),
- communication between shifts, between departments,
- attitudes toward safety in daily work,
- housekeeping, equipment condition, physical environment.

### 2.7 Closing Meeting

**Attendees:** same as opening meeting.

**Agenda:**

- Preliminary findings (no names, only patterns).
- Fact-checking (if needed).
- Timeline for final report.
- Next steps.

If the client disagrees with preliminary findings:

- The Practitioner listens and records the disagreement.
- The Practitioner does not alter findings without new evidence.
- The Practitioner cites **Code of Ethics v1.1, Section 3.4** if asked to soften conclusions.

## PHASE 3 — ANALYSIS & REPORTING

### 3.1 Data Processing

The Practitioner:

1. Consolidates interview results (anonymously).
2. Enters data into SIM Pro (or a structured spreadsheet).
3. Generates:
   - Total Structural Integrity Score (0–25),
   - Score per pillar (0–5),
   - Radar chart,
   - Identification of weakest pillars,
   - **Evidence appendix** — for every scored condition, the evidence reviewed.
4. Analyses discrepancies between respondent groups.

### 3.2 Writing the Report

The report must contain:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Executive Summary** | 1–2 pages: total score, key vulnerabilities, main recommendations. |
| **Methodology** | Brief description: who was interviewed, what documents were reviewed. |
| **Pillar-by-Pillar Analysis** | For each pillar: score, justification (quotes, observations), identified risks. |
| **Vulnerability Map** | Visualisation of where risks are concentrated. |
| **Reinforcement Plan** | Concrete steps for each weak pillar (timeline, responsible roles). |
| **Structural Risk Forecast** | What is most likely to happen if nothing changes. |
| **Evidence Appendix** | Evidence supporting each scored condition. |

**Important:** The report must not contain names, accusations, or references to specific individuals.

### 3.3 Presenting Results to the Client

**Formats:**

- Meeting (online or in person) to present key findings.
- Delivery of the report (PDF).
- Discussion of the reinforcement plan.

### 3.4 Post-Report Phase

After delivery:

- The Practitioner preserves the record for the required retention period (Code of Ethics v1.1, Section 5.4).
- The Practitioner may be contacted for follow-up audits (6–12 months).
- The Practitioner must not share the report with third parties without written consent (Code of Practice v1.1, Section 9.3).

## PHASE 4 — ETHICAL OBLIGATIONS

Every AVCS Practitioner must adhere to the Code of Ethics:

- Do not disclose confidential information.
- Do not use data to evaluate individuals.
- Do not accept compensation tied to audit outcomes.
- Protect respondent anonymity.
- Base conclusions on facts, not interpretations.
- **Require evidence, not accept status.**
- **Record convergence, not absorb it.**

---

## PHASE 5 — PRACTITIONER SELF-APPLICATION

A Practitioner applies to themselves the same structural integrity principles they impose on audited systems.

This means:

- The Practitioner's own findings are subject to evidence verification.
- The Practitioner's own assumptions are distinguished from facts.
- The Practitioner's own conclusions are provisional until tested.
- The Practitioner records divergence between findings and expectations as a **test result** — not as an automatic adjustment.

This is required by:

- **Charter v1.1, Sections 4.1–4.2**
- **CORE v2.1, Law 23**
- **Leadership Doctrine v1.1, Section 7**
- **Leadership Code v1.1, Section 7**
